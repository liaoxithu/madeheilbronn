Vision & introduction

Understanding the richness of production scheduling.

Many established benchmarks used to develop and compare production scheduling algorithms (e.g., XXX) rely on artificially generated data with convenient but potentially unrealistic distributions, such as uniformly distributed processing times. Although these benchmarks are valuable for controlled comparisons, they may overlook data patterns and problem characteristics commonly found in real-world factories, potentially steering algorithm development away from practical needs.

Scheduling problems such as makespan minimization in general job shops are computationally challenging: they are NP-hard in the strong sense (Blazewicz et al., 2019; Pinedo, 2022). No single algorithm is likely to perform well across all possible instances of a given problem. Algorithm performance depends on instance characteristics, meaning that some approaches may be substantially more effective than others for particular production environments.

Our aim is to bridge the gap between benchmark design and industrial practice. Through detailed discussions with engineers and production managers, we identify recurring data patterns and scheduling characteristics in real-world factories. These insights can guide the design and evaluation of algorithms, helping researchers develop planning methods that perform reliably in practical production settings.

The MADEHeilbronn initiative brings together researchers and manufacturers to develop realistic, well-understood datasets for research in production scheduling optimization.
